# Product overview

## Problem

Useful ideas disappear in private notes, abandoned repositories, unfinished sketches, failed prototypes, and conversations. Existing portfolio and social platforms reward finished work, while marketplaces often reduce ideas to transactions. There is little room to explain why an idea stalled and invite a responsible continuation.

## Vision

Idea Afterlife is a public archive and revival network for ideas that did not reach their intended form. It values the story, evidence, and lessons behind an idea—not only its commercial potential.

## Audiences

- Creators releasing an idea they cannot currently pursue
- Builders looking for meaningful starting points
- Researchers learning from failed attempts
- Collaborators seeking work with existing context
- Visitors browsing speculative art, technology, and social innovation

## Initial categories

Art, technology, product, story, research, community, invention, and other.

## Idea record

An idea should eventually include:

- Title and short summary
- Full story and original intention
- Why it stalled
- Remains: media, files, links, or notes
- What was tried and learned
- Last wish
- Category and tags
- Creator credit
- Permission mode
- Lifecycle status
- Linked revivals and lineage

## Permission modes

- **View only** — shared as a story; no reuse invitation
- **Inspiration welcome** — learn from it, but do not copy assets
- **Remix allowed** — derivative work is welcomed with credit
- **Collaboration wanted** — contact the creator before proceeding
- **Available for adoption** — creator welcomes an independent continuation

These labels communicate intent; they are not a substitute for licensing or legal advice.

## Non-goals for the first release

- Selling ownership of ideas
- Patent or copyright adjudication
- Private messaging
- Algorithmic popularity ranking
- AI-generated idea scoring
- Production-scale media processing
